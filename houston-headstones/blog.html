Common Mistakes Families Make When Ordering a Headstone

July 22, 2026

Ordering a headstone is rarely something families do often. A few small oversights can lead to delays, extra cost, or a design that does not feel quite right. Here are the most common mistakes we see — and how to avoid them.

  1. Skipping the cemetery rules. Almost every cemetery has size, height, material, or foundation requirements. Designing first and checking rules later often means starting over.
  2. Waiting too long to begin. Production and installation typically take several weeks after design approval. Starting the process early reduces stress around important dates.
  3. Choosing a design that is too busy. Too many symbols, photos, or long inscriptions can make lettering hard to read from a distance and feel crowded over time.
  4. Focusing only on the lowest price. Extremely low quotes sometimes omit foundation work, delivery, or proper installation. Clear written pricing protects everyone.
  5. Not reviewing a design proof carefully. Names, dates, and spellings should be checked by more than one family member. Changes are easy on a proof and expensive after the stone is cut.
  6. Ignoring the base and installation. A beautiful headstone on an uneven or inadequate foundation can lean or settle. Proper setting is part of a lasting memorial.

We created our free design proof process specifically to help families catch these issues early. When you share the cemetery name and any preferences, we can guide the design within the rules from the start so the final memorial feels both personal and practical.

Meaningful Epitaphs & Inscription Ideas for Granite Headstones

June 18, 2026

The words on a headstone become a permanent part of how someone is remembered. Many families feel pressure to find the “perfect” phrase. The truth is that simple, sincere wording often works best.

Here are categories and examples that families frequently choose:

Classic short phrases

  • In Loving Memory
  • Forever in Our Hearts
  • Gone But Not Forgotten
  • Beloved Husband, Father, and Friend
  • Rest in Peace

Faith-based options

  • The Lord is my shepherd
  • I am the resurrection and the life
  • Blessed are the pure in heart
  • Until we meet again

Personal and gentle

  • A life well lived
  • Loved beyond words
  • Her/His laughter remains
  • Always in our hearts
  • Too well loved to ever be forgotten

Tips we share with families: keep the main inscription short so it remains readable from a few feet away; leave space for names and dates to stay clear; and choose words that still feel true years from now. A free design proof lets you see the lettering on the actual granite layout before anything is engraved.

Upright vs Flat vs Slant Markers: Which Style Is Right?

May 20, 2026

Most cemeteries allow at least one of three common granite styles. Understanding the differences helps families choose a memorial that fits both the cemetery rules and the way they want the grave to feel.

Upright headstones stand vertically and are the most traditional look. They offer the largest surface for names, dates, inscriptions, and symbols. Many families prefer them because they are easy to find and read from a distance. They usually require a proper foundation or base.

Flat markers (also called flush or grass markers) sit level with or slightly above the ground. They are often required in certain sections of cemeteries for maintenance reasons. Flat markers still allow clear lettering and simple designs while keeping a low profile.

Slant markers sit at an angle, usually between 16 and 24 inches high at the back. They provide more visible surface than a flat marker without the full height of an upright. Some families like the balance of presence and practicality.

The best choice depends on the cemetery’s regulations, the size of the plot, budget, and personal preference. When you tell us the cemetery name, we can confirm which styles are allowed and show realistic examples of each so the decision feels clearer.

Choosing Granite Colors for a Headstone

April 14, 2026

Granite comes in a wide range of natural colors. The color you choose affects how the lettering and any artwork will look, how the stone ages in Houston’s sun and rain, and the overall feeling of the memorial.

Black granite remains one of the most popular choices. It offers strong contrast for light lettering and photographs, and it maintains a formal, timeless appearance. Polished black also shows detail clearly.

Gray granite is classic and blends well in many older cemetery sections. It feels traditional and understated. Lettering contrast is good, especially with darker or lighter finishes.

Pink and red granites bring warmth. Many families choose these for their softer visual tone. They can be especially meaningful for women or for those who preferred warmer colors in life.

Other options include blue-gray, brown, and multi-colored granites. What matters most is how the finished lettering and any symbols will read against the stone, and whether the color still feels right after many years outdoors.

Our interactive designer lets you try different granite colors with sample text so you can see the contrast before deciding. We are happy to walk through real samples and photos as well.

Headstone Prices in Houston TX: 2026 Guide

March 25, 2026

Prices for custom headstones in the Houston area vary based on size, style, material, engraving complexity, and cemetery requirements. As a starting point for 2026:

  • Single upright or standard markers often begin around $899–$1,299
  • Companion (double) headstones typically start near $1,599–$1,999
  • Flat markers and smaller tablets can start lower
  • Memorial benches commonly begin around $1,499–$1,799

These are starting ranges. Final cost depends on exact size, number of photos or vases, special artwork, and delivery or installation needs. We always provide a written quote and a free design proof before any deposit is requested so families can decide with clear information.

The biggest cost drivers are usually size, complexity of engraving, and any special cemetery foundation requirements. Asking about the cemetery rules early helps keep the final number predictable.

How Long Does a Custom Headstone Take?

March 8, 2026

One of the most common questions we hear is about timing. Families want to know when a memorial will be ready so they can plan visits or special dates. Here is a realistic overview of the process.

  1. Design and proof (usually 1–2 weeks). We create a layout based on your preferences, cemetery rules, and any symbols or photos. You review and approve the free design proof. Changes are easy at this stage.
  2. Production (typically 4–8 weeks after approval). Once the proof is approved and the order is placed, the granite is prepared, shaped, polished, and engraved. Exact time depends on complexity and current workload.
  3. Foundation and installation. Many cemeteries require a concrete foundation. Scheduling the setting work can add a short additional window depending on the cemetery’s calendar and weather.

Overall, most custom granite headstones are completed and installed within roughly 6–12 weeks after the design is approved. Starting the conversation early removes pressure and allows time for thoughtful decisions. If you have a specific date in mind, tell us and we will be transparent about what is realistic.

Why Granite Is the Best Choice for Headstones in Houston

January 22, 2026

Houston’s climate — intense heat, humidity, heavy rain, and occasional storms — places real demands on outdoor stone. Granite has proven to be the most reliable material for lasting memorials in our area.

Durability. Granite is an extremely hard natural stone. It resists cracking, chipping, and weathering far better than softer stones such as marble. Inscriptions stay clear for generations instead of fading or wearing away.

Weather resistance. Polished granite sheds water and does not absorb moisture the way porous materials can. It holds up well against Houston’s heat and the occasional freeze-thaw cycle that can damage lesser stones.

Low maintenance. Most granite memorials need only occasional gentle cleaning. Harsh chemicals and aggressive pressure washing are unnecessary and can be avoided.

Color and design options. Granite is available in many natural colors and takes engraving, etching, and ceramic photos cleanly. Families can create a design that feels personal without sacrificing longevity.

As a company that began with engineered stone and expanded into granite memorials because of strong local demand, we have seen firsthand how well quality granite performs here. When families want a memorial that will still look dignified decades from now, granite is the material we recommend.

Navigating Cemetery Regulations for Your Custom Headstone

March 1, 2025

Almost every cemetery has rules about size, height, thickness, foundation, and sometimes material or color. These rules exist for maintenance and appearance reasons, and they must be followed for the memorial to be installed without problems.

When you share the name of the cemetery with us, we can often confirm the main requirements and design within those limits from the beginning. This simple step prevents the frustration of a beautiful design that later needs expensive changes.

If you are unsure of the rules, we are happy to help you find them or contact the cemetery office. Taking a few minutes early protects both your time and your investment.

Designing a Headstone: 5 Key Considerations

July 15, 2025

Creating a custom headstone is a meaningful process. Five practical points we discuss with most families:

  1. Material and durability — Choose something that will hold up to Houston heat, humidity, and rain for decades.
  2. Personalization — Names, dates, a short inscription, symbols, or a photo that feel right for the person being honored.
  3. Cemetery rules — Size, height, and foundation requirements must be met.
  4. Installation and stability — Proper base or footer work keeps the memorial level and secure.
  5. Budget and timeline — Clear pricing and a realistic production window (typically 6–10 weeks after approval) reduce stress.

We guide families through these points calmly so the final memorial feels both personal and practical.

How to Maintain Your Headstone for Long-Lasting Beauty

May 10, 2025

Polished granite is naturally durable, but a little care keeps it looking its best. In Houston’s climate we recommend:

  • Gentle cleaning with water and a soft cloth or non-abrasive brush when needed
  • Avoiding harsh chemicals or pressure washers
  • Checking the base or footing occasionally to make sure it remains level
  • Clearing grass or debris that can hold moisture against the stone

Most families find that occasional light cleaning is all that is required for the memorial to stay clear and respectful for generations.
